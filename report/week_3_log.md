# Objectives

The primary objective of Week 3 was to extend the customer retention analysis by introducing financial metrics that measure customer value. The focus was on calculating Average Order Value (AOV), Purchase Frequency, and Historical Customer Lifetime Value (CLTV). Additionally, customers were segmented by acquisition channel and geographic location to identify the most valuable customer groups.

These analyses provide valuable insights for marketing, product, and finance teams by identifying customer segments that contribute the greatest long-term value.

## 3. Calculated Average Order Value (AOV)

Average Order Value (AOV) was calculated for each customer segment using total revenue divided by the total number of unique orders.

The metric measures the average amount spent during a single transaction and provides insight into customer purchasing behavior.

**Status:** Completed

---

## 4. Calculated Purchase Frequency

Purchase Frequency was calculated by dividing the total number of unique orders by the number of unique customers within each segment.

This metric measures how frequently customers make purchases and is an important indicator of customer engagement and loyalty.

**Status:** Completed

---

## 5. Calculated Historical Customer Lifetime Value (CLTV)

Historical Customer Lifetime Value (CLTV) was estimated using the calculated Average Order Value and Purchase Frequency.

The resulting CLTV values provide an estimate of the revenue generated by customers within each segment over their historical purchasing activity.

**Status:** Completed

# Challenges Encountered

## Challenge 1: Selecting an Appropriate CLTV Formula

Customer Lifetime Value can be calculated using several different methods depending on the available data.

**Issue**

The dataset did not contain information such as customer lifespan, subscription duration, or future purchase probabilities required for predictive CLTV models.

**Resolution**

A historical CLTV approach was adopted using Average Order Value and Purchase Frequency. This approach aligns with the available transactional data and satisfies the project requirements.

---

## Challenge 2: Comparing Customer Segments

Customer segments varied significantly in terms of size and transaction volume.

**Resolution**

Normalized metrics such as Average Order Value and Purchase Frequency were calculated to enable fair comparisons across acquisition channels and geographic regions.
